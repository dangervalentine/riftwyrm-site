# Research: Strategy Guide

The source is the private report *Riftwyrm Playtest Round 2* (updated 29 Oct 2026) and the game
repo `../dragon-hunter` (`data/cards.json`, `data/text.json`, `data/deck_guide.json`,
`data/advisor_showdown.json`, `data/advisor_value.json`, `build/card-shots/adept/`).

## R1: Where the numbers come from

**Decision**: There are two sources.

- **Generated by `sync-assets.mjs`** (`guideStats` in `media.generated.ts`):
  - deck targets: `deck_guide.json` → `content.classic.dragon_*.{even, likely}`;
  - dragon Speed and First Strike: `cards.json` → `dragons[].initiative`, `first_strike.value`;
  - each Adept Card's id, name, Essence and strength at Levels 1–4, with each step's XP cost.
    Names come from `text.json` `card.*`.`official`. Strength is `max(basic.value,
    enhanced.value) + boost` and the cost is `level_up_cost` at the lower Level. Spot-checked
    against the report: Static Rush (Fire) L1 = 5, and 1→2 is +5 for 1 XP ✓.
- **Hand-written in `components/guide.ts`**:
  - win rates by difficulty;
  - the raise and pass picks and their reasons;
  - dragon attack facts used as tips (e.g. Cindermaw hits 24 at the middle Grade);
  - the lessons and the edition line.

**Rationale**: Deck targets, Speed and card strength are game data that change with a card set.
Generating them keeps the page honest after an expansion (US5), and the sync script already reads
`cards.json` for `regionStats`. Win rates and picks exist only in the report, so they are written by
hand.

**Alternatives considered**:
- Everything by hand: simpler, but the guide could drift from the game silently.
- Deriving the picks from `advisor_*.json` automatically: the picks need editorial reconciling of
  the two rankings (R3), and an automatic ranking would also surface noise-level differences
  (spec edge case).

## R3: Reconciling the report's two card rankings

**Decision**:
- **Raise** = top of the deck-outcome ranking, with no low rating in the Showdown ranking. Lead with
  cards that are top-three in both.
- **Pass on** = low in both rankings. Below +2.5 by deck outcome is "low", and so is being among the
  Showdown solving's two least valuable cards.
- A card the rankings disagree on is neither. It may get a "good, not first" note.

The picks below follow the spec's Source facts table:

| Dragon | Raise (lead first) | Pass on | Disputed (no list) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Thrallis | C16 Static Rush (Fire), C06 Lightning Step (Lightning), C04 Cold Blaze (Water), C09 Swift Current (Shadow) | C03 Scorch (Water), C02 Blinding Blade (Water), C07 Surge (Lightning) | C15 Ember Dash (Fire), C12 Lightning Step (Shadow) |

## R4: Win-rate figures to publish

**Decision**: Publish the strong player that sees only what a player sees ("a strong player"), for
all six difficulties × four dragons, as a compact table with inline bars. Mention the all-knowing
ceiling once, for Dire Cindermaw (about 78%). Round to whole percent.

**Rationale**: FR-009. This is the honest picture of what good play achieves. The rule-based and
random players would confuse readers.

**Figures** (strong player, %):

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Thrallis | 99 | 97 | 88 | 94 | 81 | 68 |
| Glaciarch | 99 | 98 | 95 | 92 | 79 | 73 |
| Cindermaw | 95 | 88 | 72 | 60 | 40 | 25 |

## R5: Lessons and how strongly to state them

**Decision**: Eight lessons. Each has a plain instruction, a reason, an evidence tag, and an effect
in words where one was measured:

1. **The Regions are deck building.** Good play reached the Showdown every time. (Observed)
2. **Empower the biggest strength jump**, even at 3–5 XP. It won about 12 more games in 100.
   (Tested)
3. **Weigh the dragon you face** when Empowering and Bracing. About 4 more in 100, and better on
   every dragon. The exception is Glaciarch, where the biggest jump is already right. (Tested)
4. **Brace with the fewest cards**, and keep Level 1 cards out of the Trash. Among those, give up the
   Levels this dragon needs least, which is the pass list. (Tested)
5. **Detour when your best Play is weak**, at most twice a Trial. About 3 more in 100. (Tested)
6. **Don't Meld just for the enhancement.** Favouring it cost about 11 games in 100. Judge a Meld by
   what it wins and costs, and remember the tucked card is spent. (Tested: a "don't")
7. **Don't chase Speed when Empowering, but do Boost Speed in the Reckoning** when that lifts you
   past the dragon's Speed. Chasing Speed cost about 4 in 100. A strong deck brings Speed with it.
   (Tested + observed)
8. **Rally on the Approach** when the seven cards hold your hardest hitters or lack your movers.
   Approach cards are discarded whatever you play. (Observed)

Also: **Empowering your strongest card is a myth**. It costs about 10 in 100 against the biggest
jump. This folds into lesson 2.

**Excluded**:
- The rules dead end (two Detours leaving no Hand). It was seen only in random play, it is a rules
  question rather than a tip, and it is not shown to players.
- Round 1 comparisons, advice-tool timings and phone checks.

## R6: Editorial form

**Decision**: Long-form article pages in a column wider than `Prose` (about 72ch body, with
full-bleed dragon art headers):

- a display-serif headline, a standfirst and a byline/edition line;
- a drop cap on the opening paragraph;
- pull-out "Rule of thumb" tips;
- on each dragon page, a stat strip (Deck · Likely-win deck · Speed · First Strike), then Raise and
  Pass on as two short lists of card chips (face thumbnail, name, Essence, one-line reason).

The main guide page uses the same header and column, and four dragon cards as its index.

**Rationale**: The user asked for an "editorial article". The site's palette and fonts already read
as a printed field guide.

**Alternatives considered**:
- Reuse `Prose` as is: too narrow for card chips and art headers.
- Tabs on one page: needs client JS and hides content (superseded by the five-page split, R10).

## R7: Tables at 360 px

**Decision**:
- **Glance and win-rate tables**: real `<table>` elements with a caption, inside a wrapper. Below
  640 px they reflow with CSS into one block per dragon, with `data-label` cells shown through
  `::before`.
- **Empower reference**: one row per card, with Levels as compact `5 → 10 → 13 → 17` sequences and
  step costs under each arrow.

**Rationale**: FR-015, without client JS, and the semantic table is kept.

## R8: Card art

**Decision**: The sync script copies all 16 Adept faces from `build/card-shots/adept/NN_Cxx.png`
to `public/media/cards/adept-<slug>-<essence>.webp`, at the existing 480 px card width. The id is
read from the file name, so the `NN_` order prefix is ignored. The existing six
`adept-<slug>.webp` files stay because the home page uses them. Chips show faces at about 56 px,
lazy-loaded, with `alt=""` because the name is in the text.

## R9: Discoverability

**Decision**:
- Header link "Guide" and footer link "Strategy guide", both to `/guide`.
- One line in the home page's road section.
- All five pages in the sitemap.
- Each page gets its own title, description, canonical and share image.
- `Article` JSON-LD on each page, with the author from `site.developer` and `dateModified` from
  `edition.updated`.
- `BreadcrumbList` JSON-LD on dragon pages (Guide → Dragon).
- Lessons keep anchors (`/guide#lesson-<id>`) so dragon pages can link to the relevant lesson.

## R10: Five pages from one template

**Decision**:
- `app/guide/[dragon]/page.tsx` with `generateStaticParams()` returning the four slugs and
  `export const dynamicParams = false`.
- `generateMetadata({ params })` awaits `params` (a Promise in Next 16).
- **Slugs** are the dragon's name in lower case (`thrallis`, `glaciarch`, `cindermaw`,
  `vesperath`). They are derived from `content.ts`'s `dragons` by one helper in `guide.ts`. That
  helper is the only place a slug is made.
- **Order** follows unlock order. Previous/next wrap around: Vesperath's "next" is Thrallis.

**Rationale**: One template guarantees every dragon page has the same anatomy (SC-004). A closed
param set keeps the pages fully static (Principle III). Name slugs read well in shared links and
search ("riftwyrm.…/guide/cindermaw").

**Alternatives considered**:
- Four hand-written page files: they would duplicate layout and drift.
- Essence slugs (`/guide/fire`): less memorable, and the game shows dragons by name first.

## R11: Share images per page

**Decision**:
- `app/guide/opengraph-image.tsx` draws the four dragon heads over the ground with the guide's
  title.
- `app/guide/[dragon]/opengraph-image.tsx` draws that dragon over its lair with "How to beat
  <Name>", in the same composition as the home page's image.
- Both reuse `palette` and the Spectral Bold font file.
- The renderer cannot read WebP, so `sync-assets` writes PNGs for all four dragons to
  `assets/og/<id>-lair.png` and `assets/og/<id>-dragon.png` at the existing sizes.
- `twitter-image.tsx` re-exports in both places, as on the home page.

**Rationale**: FR-018 and US4 scenario 3, each dragon page's preview showing that dragon.
